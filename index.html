WHAT'S UNDER THE HOOD

Not all miles feel the same.

SurfaceStride builds running routes using walkable ways in OpenStreetMap. It reads tags such as surface=grass, surface=gravel, and surface=asphalt, then searches for connected routes close to your target distance.

Surface confidence matters. Explicitly tagged surfaces are distinguished from inferred pavement and unknown paths. A green line is only as reliable as its map data.

Use the Google Earth link and downloadable KML to inspect routes in satellite imagery, or use Street View checkpoints to visually confirm segments where imagery exists. The app does not scrape Google images or automatically classify their pixels.

Elevation estimates use Open-Meteo terrain height samples (Copernicus GLO-90 DEM). Flat, Rolling, and Hilly compare candidate routes by climbing; a flat preference cannot guarantee no hills. Elevation gain, grades, and exported altitudes are estimates, especially for short climbs, bridges, and tunnels. If the elevation provider is unavailable the surface route still works.

You do not need to provide your exact starting point. Pan to an area of interest and SurfaceStride automatically selects a nearby mapped walking path as the origin of each route.

Most trails, grassy fields, and private tracks don't have complete surface tags; routes can also contain intersections or roads that require safety judgment. Treat generated routes as suggestions, not turn-by-turn navigation.
